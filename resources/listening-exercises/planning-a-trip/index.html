Planning a Trip

An intermediate-level planning conversation. Learners hear modals of suggestion ('we could…', 'let's…'), money expressions, and travel vocabulary (itinerary, budget, accommodation).

Level B1 Travel & Directions ⏱ 3–4 minutes

Vocabulary

itinerary a detailed plan of a journey, including places and times
budget the amount of money you can spend on something
accommodation a place to stay, like a hotel or hostel
book in advance to arrange something before you need it
sightseeing visiting interesting places as a tourist

Dialogue

Speaker 1 So, Emma, are you ready to plan our trip to Japan?
Speaker 2 Almost. We have two weeks and a budget of two thousand euros each.
Speaker 1 Perfect. I think we should book accommodation in advance. Hotels fill up quickly.
Speaker 2 Good idea. Maybe we can stay in small guesthouses to save money.
Speaker 1 I agree. What about the itinerary? I would love to visit Kyoto.
Speaker 2 Kyoto is a must. We could spend four days there, doing some sightseeing.
Speaker 1 And we should keep a couple of free days for spontaneous plans.
Speaker 2 Exactly. We could also take a day trip to Nara if we have time.
Speaker 1 Sounds great. What about packing?
Speaker 2 Light clothes, comfortable shoes, and a small backpack for day trips.

Comprehension Questions

  1. One week
  2. Two weeks
  3. Three weeks

2. How much is the budget per person?
  1. One thousand euros
  2. Two thousand euros
  3. Three thousand euros
Two thousand euros
3. Which city is a 'must' according to Emma?
  1. Tokyo
  2. Osaka
  3. Kyoto
Kyoto

True / False

They plan to book hotels at the last minute.
False — The speaker says 'we should book accommodation in advance.'
Emma suggests staying in small guesthouses.
True — Emma says 'Maybe we can stay in small guesthouses to save money.'

Teacher Tips

  • Pre-teach 'we could…' and 'let's…' as planning phrases. Recycle modal verbs of suggestion.
  • After listening, students plan a short trip in pairs using the same structure (where, how long, budget, must-see places).
  • Extension: present the plan to the class in a one-minute talk.

Planificar un viaje

Una conversación intermedia de planificación. Los estudiantes oyen modales de sugerencia ('we could…', 'let's…'), expresiones de dinero y vocabulario de viaje (itinerary, budget, accommodation).

Nivel B1 Viajes y direcciones ⏱ 3–4 minutes

Vocabulario

itinerary un plan detallado de un viaje, con lugares y horarios
budget la cantidad de dinero que puedes gastar en algo
accommodation un lugar donde alojarse, como un hotel o un albergue
book in advance reservar algo antes de que lo necesites
sightseeing visitar lugares de interés como turista

Diálogo

Speaker 1 So, Emma, are you ready to plan our trip to Japan?
Speaker 2 Almost. We have two weeks and a budget of two thousand euros each.
Speaker 1 Perfect. I think we should book accommodation in advance. Hotels fill up quickly.
Speaker 2 Good idea. Maybe we can stay in small guesthouses to save money.
Speaker 1 I agree. What about the itinerary? I would love to visit Kyoto.
Speaker 2 Kyoto is a must. We could spend four days there, doing some sightseeing.
Speaker 1 And we should keep a couple of free days for spontaneous plans.
Speaker 2 Exactly. We could also take a day trip to Nara if we have time.
Speaker 1 Sounds great. What about packing?
Speaker 2 Light clothes, comfortable shoes, and a small backpack for day trips.

  1. Una semana

  3. Tres semanas

2. ¿Cuánto es el presupuesto por persona?
  1. Mil euros
  2. Dos mil euros
  3. Tres mil euros
Dos mil euros
3. ¿Qué ciudad es, según Emma, una visita obligada?
  1. Tokio
  2. Osaka
  3. Kioto
Kioto

Verdadero / Falso

Planean reservar hoteles a última hora.
Falso — El interlocutor dice 'we should book accommodation in advance'.
Emma sugiere alojarse en pequeños hostales.
Verdadero — Emma dice 'Maybe we can stay in small guesthouses to save money'.

Consejos para el docente

  • Enseña antes 'we could…' y 'let's…' como frases para planificar. Recicla los verbos modales de sugerencia.
  • Después de escuchar, los estudiantes planifican un viaje corto por parejas usando la misma estructura (dónde, cuánto tiempo, presupuesto, lugares imprescindibles).
  • Ampliación: presentan el plan a la clase en una exposición de un minuto.
