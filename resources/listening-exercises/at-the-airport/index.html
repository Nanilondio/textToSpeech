At the Airport

An elementary travel scenario covering check-in vocabulary: boarding pass, hand luggage, gate, departure time, and a short security question.

Level A2 Airport & Flights ⏱ 2 minutes

Vocabulary

check-in desk the counter at an airport where you register for your flight
boarding pass the card that lets you get on the plane
hand luggage the small bag you carry with you on the plane
departure the act of leaving, especially on a flight
gate the door at an airport where you get on the plane

Dialogue

Speaker 1 Good morning. May I see your passport, please?
Speaker 2 Here you are. I am flying to Berlin.
Speaker 1 Thank you. Do you have any luggage to check in?
Speaker 2 Yes, one suitcase. And I have this small bag as hand luggage.
Speaker 1 Perfect. Please put the suitcase on the scale.
Speaker 2 Sure. What is the departure time?
Speaker 1 Your flight leaves at eleven thirty from gate B twelve.
Speaker 2 And what time do I need to be at the gate?
Speaker 1 Please be at the gate at least thirty minutes before departure.

Comprehension Questions

1. Where is the passenger flying to?
  1. London
  2. Berlin
  3. Paris
Berlin
2. How many suitcases does the passenger check in?
  1. None
  2. One
  3. Two
One
3. What is the gate number?
  1. B ten
  2. B twelve
  3. B twenty
B twelve

True / False

The passenger only has hand luggage.
False — The passenger checks in one suitcase and also has a hand-luggage bag.
The flight leaves at eleven thirty.
True — The agent says 'Your flight leaves at eleven thirty from gate B twelve.'

Teacher Tips

  • Before listening, show a real boarding pass or print a sample so students recognise key fields (gate, seat, boarding time).
  • Practise 'May I see your passport, please?' and similar polite airport phrases as a fixed exchange.
  • Extension: students design their own airport role-play with check-in, security, and the gate.

En el aeropuerto

Un escenario de viaje elemental con vocabulario de facturación: boarding pass, hand luggage, gate, departure time, y una breve pregunta de seguridad.

Nivel A2 Aeropuerto y vuelos ⏱ 2 minutes

Vocabulario

check-in desk el mostrador del aeropuerto donde te registras para tu vuelo
boarding pass la tarjeta que te permite subir al avión
hand luggage la bolsa pequeña que llevas contigo en el avión
departure el acto de irse, especialmente en un vuelo
gate la puerta del aeropuerto donde subes al avión

Diálogo

Speaker 1 Good morning. May I see your passport, please?
Speaker 2 Here you are. I am flying to Berlin.
Speaker 1 Thank you. Do you have any luggage to check in?
Speaker 2 Yes, one suitcase. And I have this small bag as hand luggage.
Speaker 1 Perfect. Please put the suitcase on the scale.
Speaker 2 Sure. What is the departure time?
Speaker 1 Your flight leaves at eleven thirty from gate B twelve.
Speaker 2 And what time do I need to be at the gate?
Speaker 1 Please be at the gate at least thirty minutes before departure.

Preguntas de comprensión

1. ¿Adónde vuela el pasajero?
  1. Londres
  2. Berlín
  3. París
Berlín
2. ¿Cuántas maletas factura el pasajero?
  1. Ninguna
  2. Una
  3. Dos
Una
3. ¿Cuál es el número de puerta?
  1. B diez
  2. B doce
  3. B veinte
B doce

Verdadero / Falso

El pasajero solo lleva equipaje de mano.
Falso — El pasajero factura una maleta y también lleva una bolsa de mano.
El vuelo sale a las once y media.
Verdadero — El agente dice 'Your flight leaves at eleven thirty from gate B twelve'.

Consejos para el docente

  • Antes de escuchar, muestra una tarjeta de embarque real o imprime una de ejemplo para que los estudiantes reconozcan los campos clave (gate, seat, boarding time).
  • Practica 'May I see your passport, please?' y frases similares del aeropuerto como intercambio fijo.
  • Ampliación: los estudiantes diseñan su propio role-play de aeropuerto con facturación, seguridad y puerta de embarque.
