Job Interview

A B1 listening lesson about a realistic job interview. Learners follow developed answers about experience, transferable skills and problem-solving, then practise giving evidence for their own strengths.

Level B1 Work & Careers ⏱ 15–18 minutes (editorial estimate)

Vocabulary

handle enquiries deal with questions or requests from customers
change a booking make a different reservation or travel arrangement
practical solution an answer that can work in a real situation
keep track of continue to know the status or details of something
follow-up a later action to check or continue a matter
build on experience use existing knowledge as a basis for developing further skills

Context and timing

A candidate has a first interview for a customer-support position at a software company.

15–18 minutes; editorial estimate for preparation, two listens, evidence-based feedback and a speaking extension.

Learning focus

  • Identify experience, strengths and closing language in a job interview.

Before listening

  • What information might an interviewer ask about your experience?

Before listening

  1. In pairs, rank these interview topics from easiest to hardest to answer: previous experience, strengths, a difficult situation, reason for applying. Explain one choice.
  2. Predict two follow-up questions an interviewer might ask after a candidate names a strength.
  3. Match the expressions handle enquiries, keep track of, calm someone down and take responsibility to their likely workplace meaning.

First listening: general understanding

  1. Listen once and choose the best summary: A) A candidate explains her customer-support experience and how she dealt with a delayed order. B) A manager trains a new employee on software. C) Two colleagues compare their salaries.
  2. Note the position, the candidate’s previous job and her main reason for applying.

Second listening: details

  1. Complete the notes: previous role: ____; daily responsibility: ____; strength: ____; example problem: ____.
  2. Which three actions did Nina take when a customer’s order was delayed? Put them in the order she describes them.
  3. Why does Nina want to move into software support? Select the reason she gives and identify the line that supports it.
  4. Decide true or false: Nina worked in a hotel; she checked information before calling the customer back; the interviewer asks for an example of organisation.

Follow-up activity

  1. Use the structure situation–action–result to answer: Tell me about a time you solved a problem at work or in your studies. A partner listens and asks one follow-up question about the result.
  2. For a second round, choose one strength from the lesson and give a different example without repeating Nina’s situation.

Transcript

Speaker 1 Thanks for coming in today, Nina. Could you start by telling me about your previous experience?
Speaker 2 Of course. I worked in customer service for a travel company for three years. I handled enquiries by phone and email, and I helped customers change bookings when their plans changed.
Speaker 1 What did you enjoy most about that role?
Speaker 2 I liked finding practical solutions, especially when someone was worried or in a hurry. I learned to listen carefully and explain the next step clearly.
Speaker 1 This position can be busy. Can you give me an example of handling a difficult situation?
Speaker 2 Once, a customer’s train was cancelled the day before a trip. She was very upset, so I first checked the available options and spoke to the train company. Then I called her back with two possible routes and helped her choose one.
Speaker 1 What would you say is one of your main strengths?
Speaker 2 I am organised. I keep track of several cases at the same time, and I make notes so that I do not forget a follow-up. My team leader often trusted me with the more complicated enquiries.
Speaker 1 Why are you interested in moving into software support?
Speaker 2 I enjoy helping people with practical problems, and I would like to learn more about digital products. Your company also provides training, so I could build on my customer-service experience while developing new technical skills.
Speaker 1 That makes sense. Is there anything you would like to ask me?
Speaker 2 Yes. How does the team share information when a problem cannot be solved immediately?

Comprehension Questions

1. How long did Nina work in customer service?
  1. One year
  2. Three years
  3. Five years
2. What happened to the customer’s train?
  1. It was delayed for one hour
  2. It was cancelled
  3. It arrived early
3. What strength does Nina identify?
  1. Organisation
  2. Public speaking
  3. Sales

True / False

Nina worked for a travel company.
True — She says this directly when describing her previous experience.
Nina gave the customer only one possible route.
False — She called back with two possible routes.

Answer key

  1. Three years
  2. It was cancelled
  3. Organisation

Answer key

global-summary: A; Nina explains travel-company customer service and a response to a cancelled train.

The interview covers her previous customer-service role and the specific cancelled-train situation she handled.

Evidence: line 2, line 5, line 6

detailed-notes: customer service at a travel company; handling enquiries and changing bookings; organisation; a customer’s train was cancelled.

Nina names the company context and duties in line 2, her strength in line 8, and the problem in line 6.

Evidence: line 2, line 6, line 8

problem-actions: She checked available options, spoke to the train company, then called the customer with two routes and helped her choose.

These actions show how Nina moved from gathering information to offering a practical solution.

Evidence: line 6

motivation: She wants to help with practical problems while learning about digital products and developing technical skills.

Nina connects the new role to her existing customer-service experience and the company’s training.

Evidence: line 9, line 10

true-false: False; true; false.

Nina worked for a travel company, she checked options before calling back, and the interviewer asks about a difficult situation rather than organisation.

Evidence: line 2, line 6, line 8

Teacher Tips

  • Use a simple situation–action–result frame so learners hear how examples make interview answers convincing.
  • Pause after the problem-solving answer and ask which details show empathy, initiative and organisation.
  • In pair work, require one follow-up question so learners practise developing an answer rather than giving isolated sentences.

Teacher guide

Procedure

  1. Preparation (4 minutes): elicit interview topics and clarify handle enquiries, keep track of and follow-up with quick workplace examples.
  2. First listen (3 minutes): play the interview without the transcript. Learners identify the position, previous job and reason for applying.
  3. Second listen (5 minutes): learners complete notes and sequence the problem-solving actions. Ask them to underline evidence, not just answers.
  4. Feedback (3 minutes): reconstruct Nina’s answer using situation, action and result; compare why each detail is useful in an interview.
  5. Speaking extension (6 minutes): learners answer the problem question, receive one follow-up question and improve one detail.

Differentiation

  1. Support: provide a note frame with the headings role, responsibility, problem, action and result; replay lines 5–10 once.
  2. Stretch: ask learners to paraphrase Nina’s answer and add a measurable result to their own example.

Follow-up activity

  1. Learners prepare a 60-second answer to Tell me about a time you solved a problem, then record or rehearse it using situation–action–result.

Extension activity

  • Prepare and practise answers to two interview questions.

Entrevista de trabajo

Una lección B1 de comprensión auditiva sobre una entrevista de trabajo realista. El alumnado sigue respuestas desarrolladas sobre experiencia, habilidades transferibles y resolución de problemas, y practica cómo justificar sus fortalezas con ejemplos.

Nivel B1 Trabajo y profesiones ⏱ 15–18 minutos (editorial estimate)

Vocabulario

handle enquiries atender preguntas o solicitudes de clientes
change a booking modificar una reserva o gestión de viaje
practical solution una solución que funciona en una situación real
keep track of seguir conociendo el estado o los detalles de algo
follow-up acción posterior para comprobar o continuar un asunto
build on experience usar conocimientos previos como base para desarrollar nuevas habilidades

Contexto y duración

Una candidata realiza una primera entrevista para un puesto de atención al cliente en una empresa de software.

15–18 minutos; estimación editorial para la preparación, dos escuchas, la retroalimentación con evidencia y una extensión oral.

Enfoque de aprendizaje

  • Identificar experiencia, fortalezas y expresiones de cierre en una entrevista laboral.

Antes de escuchar

  • ¿Qué información puede preguntar un entrevistador sobre tu experiencia?

Antes de escuchar

  1. En parejas, ordenen estos temas de entrevista de más fácil a más difícil: experiencia, fortalezas, situación difícil y motivo para postularse. Justifiquen una elección.
  2. Predigan dos preguntas de seguimiento después de que una candidata mencione una fortaleza.
  3. Relacionen handle enquiries, keep track of, calm someone down y take responsibility con su posible significado laboral.

Primera escucha: comprensión general

  1. Escuchen una vez y elijan: A) Una candidata explica su experiencia en atención al cliente y cómo resolvió un problema con un tren cancelado. B) Un gerente forma a una persona nueva. C) Dos compañeros comparan sus sueldos.
  2. Anoten el puesto, el trabajo anterior y el motivo principal para postularse.

Segunda escucha: detalles

  1. Completen: trabajo anterior: ____; responsabilidad: ____; fortaleza: ____; problema: ____.
  2. ¿Qué tres acciones realizó Nina cuando se canceló el tren? Ordénenlas.
  3. ¿Por qué quiere pasar a soporte de software? Elijan el motivo y señalen la línea que lo demuestra.
  4. Decidan si son verdaderas: Nina trabajó en un hotel; comprobó la información antes de llamar; el entrevistador pide un ejemplo de organización.

Actividad de seguimiento

  1. Usen la estructura situación–acción–resultado para responder: Cuéntame una ocasión en la que resolviste un problema en el trabajo o los estudios. El compañero hace una pregunta de seguimiento.
  2. Para ampliar, elijan otra fortaleza y den un ejemplo diferente al de Nina.

Preguntas de comprensión

1. ¿Cuánto tiempo trabajó Nina en atención al cliente?
  1. Un año
  2. Tres años
  3. Cinco años
2. ¿Qué ocurrió con el tren de la clienta?
  1. Se retrasó una hora
  2. Se canceló
  3. Llegó antes
3. ¿Qué fortaleza menciona Nina?
  1. La organización
  2. Hablar en público
  3. Las ventas

Verdadero / Falso

Nina trabajó para una empresa de viajes.
Verdadero — Lo dice directamente al describir su experiencia anterior.
Nina ofreció a la clienta una sola ruta.
Falso — Dice que llamó con dos rutas posibles.

Respuestas

  1. Tres años
  2. Se canceló
  3. La organización

Consejos para el docente

  • Use una estructura situación–acción–resultado para mostrar cómo los ejemplos fortalecen las respuestas.
  • Pause después de la respuesta sobre el problema y pregunte qué detalles muestran empatía, iniciativa y organización.
  • Exija una pregunta de seguimiento en parejas para practicar respuestas desarrolladas.

Guía docente

Procedimiento

  1. Preparación (4 minutos): active temas de entrevista y aclare handle enquiries, keep track of y follow-up con ejemplos laborales breves.
  2. Primera escucha (3 minutos): reproduzca sin transcripción. Identifiquen puesto, trabajo anterior y motivo.
  3. Segunda escucha (5 minutos): completen las notas y ordenen las acciones; pidan subrayar evidencias.
  4. Corrección (3 minutos): reconstruya la respuesta de Nina con situación, acción y resultado; comenten por qué cada detalle es útil.
  5. Extensión oral (6 minutos): respondan la pregunta del problema, reciban una pregunta de seguimiento y mejoren un detalle.

Diferenciación

  1. Apoyo: entregue una tabla con puesto, responsabilidad, problema, acción y resultado; repita las líneas 5–10.
  2. Ampliación: parafraseen la respuesta de Nina y añadan un resultado medible a su propio ejemplo.

Actividad de seguimiento

  1. Preparen una respuesta de 60 segundos a Cuéntame una ocasión en la que resolviste un problema y ensáyenla con situación–acción–resultado.

Actividad de ampliación

  • Prepara y practica respuestas para dos preguntas de entrevista.
